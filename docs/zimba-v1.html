Zimba v1

Every workflow the app will have, and nothing else. Written as job stories, actions, and breadboards — rough enough to leave room, solved enough to build, bounded enough to finish.

Shaped against Getting Real, Rework, and Shape Up. Read time ~25 minutes.

The verdict on what exists today

4/10
today

10/10
v1

Zimba today is not badly built. It's over-built. Nineteen workspace routes, eight navigation destinations, four status fields on a single expense, three separate file-upload implementations, three ways to create the same budget category, and a settings page whose every value is hardcoded.

Why 4 and not lower: the domain modelling is real, the server actions are disciplined, error handling is typed, and there's a genuine insight buried in there — construction money is tracked per budget line, not per month. Why not higher: nothing has been said no to. Every idea that arrived got a route.

The four things holding the score down

Getting to 10 is mostly subtraction. The plan below deletes 9 of 19 routes and adds two.

Who Zimba is for, and who it isn't

Zimba is for a Ugandan construction company running 2 to 15 sites where the director's real question is: "Are we still inside budget on Kitala, and what have we actually paid Ssemwanga?"

Two roles. Not four. The current four — owner, site_manager, accountant, viewer — are three roles more than any customer has asked to distinguish, and permissions complexity is the most reliable rabbit hole in software.

RoleWhoCanCannot
Crew Foreman, site supervisor. Phone only, in the sun, one hand free. Snap receipts. See the projects they're on. See money totals. File. Pay. Delete anything.
Manager Director, accountant, owner. Phone and desk. Everything. —
Zimba is not for

Companies that need approval chains, multi-currency, purchase orders, payroll, or Quickbooks sync. We will say that on the marketing site, in those words. Turning away the wrong customer is cheaper than building for them.

One currency, decided at signup, never a setting

UGX. Whole shillings, no decimals — nobody in this market prices cement to the cent. Thousands separators appear as you type. There is no currency preference screen because a construction company does not change currency, and every preference is a decision we failed to make.

The words

The app currently uses four words for one thing and three words for another. Interface copy is the product; pick one word each and use it everywhere — code, UI, emails, support replies.

Four doors

Eight navigation destinations become four. Same four on desktop and phone, in the same order, so the app is one app.

  Inbox        receipts that still need filing        ← the new home
  Projects     money per project, per budget line
  Suppliers    who we owe, what we've paid
  Team         who's in here

  Account menu (bottom left / top right)
    Company name · Your name · Sign out

Crew see two doors, not four: a full-screen Add receipt button and Projects. No inbox, no totals. The interface tells them their whole job.

Why Inbox is home and not a dashboard

The current home shows three stat cards, a project list, and a recent-activity feed. It answers no question and prompts no action. A dashboard with widgets is something you look at; an inbox is something you empty. Zimba's home should be a to-do, and when it's empty it should say so and get out of the way.

Getting in

W1

Join ZimbaSmall batch · 1–2 weeks

Manager · phone or desk · once
Job story

When I hear Zimba might fix my receipt problem, I want to be looking at my own project inside two minutes, so I can judge it on my site instead of on a sales call.

What happens today

Daniel registers. He's bounced to /workspace, then /onboarding, fills in a company form, and lands on /pending-approval. Now a human being has to open a second application and approve him. He doesn't know that. He waits. Nobody tells him how long because nobody knows. He closes the tab.

Time to first value: unbounded. This is the single most expensive thing in the product.

Actions

  • Create an account with email and password
  • Name the company
  • Land inside, signed in, workspace ready

                          Region, fiscal period, plot size: not asked.
  "Start"  ─────────────────────────►  [Inbox — empty]

[Inbox — empty]
  "Nothing to file yet."
  "Start a project"  ───────────────►  W3

Three fields, then one field, then you're in. The onboarding form's other questions were never used by any downstream screen.

Rabbit hole

Slug collisions. Two companies called "Zimba Consultants" both want /zimba-consultants. Append a short suffix silently and never show the user a slug field. Nobody has ever wanted to choose a URL slug for their construction company.

No-gos
  • No admin approval step. Signup is self-serve or this pitch is pointless.
  • No email verification gate before you can use the app — verify in the background, block nothing.
  • No Google or Apple sign-in in v1.
  • No plan selection, no trial countdown, no credit card.
  • No sample or demo data. Real emptiness with a clear next step beats fake projects you have to delete.

Invite the crewSmall batch · 1–2 weeks

Manager · desk · occasionally
Job story

When a foreman starts on the Kitala site, I want him sending me receipts by this afternoon, so three weeks of paper doesn't pile up in his pocket while we do paperwork.

What happens today

Daniel opens Team, clicks Invite member, an inline form unfolds, and he's asked to choose between "Site manager", "Accountant", "Viewer", and "Owner" — four options where he only has two kinds of people. Then Zimba emails a link. Musa the foreman does not use email. He uses WhatsApp.

Actions

  • Add someone as Crew or Manager
  • Copy their invite link and send it however you already talk to them
  • See who hasn't accepted yet, and re-copy the link
  • Remove someone

Interaction

[Team]
  list of people — name, Crew or Manager
  pending invites — "Musa · link not used yet"  ·  [Copy link]  ·  [Remove]
  "Add someone"  ──────────────────►  [Add someone]

[Add someone]
  Name
  Crew  ●───○  Manager        ← two choices, defaulting to Crew
  "Create invite"  ────────────────►  [Invite ready]

[Invite ready]

  zimba.app/i/8fk2m           [Copy link]   ← primary action
  "Email it instead"           ← secondary, quiet
  "Done"  ─────────────────────────►  [Team]

The primary action is copy a link, not send an email. This is deliberate underdoing: WhatsApp is the messaging infrastructure of this market and we don't have to build any of it. Email is the fallback, not the default.

Rabbit hole

Invite link security. A copyable link that anyone can forward needs a real expiry and single use. 14 days, one use, and the name is baked in so the wrong person accepting is visible immediately.

No-gos
  • No bulk invite, no CSV, no paste-a-list-of-emails.
  • No SMS sending in v1 — no gateway, no delivery reports, no per-message cost.
  • No per-project permissions. Crew see every project. If that's wrong, we'll hear about it.
  • No role changes after invite. Remove and re-add. It happens twice a year.

Rabbit hole

The draft machinery has to go, not be fixed. The two-page wizard needed localStorage to carry state across a navigation. One page needs no draft, no readProjectCreateDraft, no clearProjectCreateDraft, and the bug disappears by deletion rather than by patch. Do not port the draft code forward.

No-gos

  • No file upload during creation.

  • No client records. A client name is a text field on the project, if we add it at all.
W4

Change the budgetSmall batch · 1–2 weeks

Manager · desk · monthly
Job story

When the roofing quote comes back 40% over what I guessed, I want to move money between budget lines and add the line I forgot, so the numbers on screen match the job I'm actually running.

What happens today

Three separate ways to create the same thing: a whole page at /projects/[id]/tasks/new, a dialog triggered by a magic __create_new_category__ value inside a dropdown during receipt entry, and the rows during project creation. All three call the same action. Each is worded differently — task, category, allocation.

Actions

  • Add a budget line, inline, wherever you notice it's missing
  • Change a line's amount or name in place
  • Delete a line that has no receipts against it

Interaction

[Project ▸ Budget]
  Foundation      12,000,000    spent 9,120,000    ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░  76%
  Walling          8,500,000    spent 1,200,000    ▓░░░░░░░░░  14%
  + Add line                    ← inline row, appears in place, saves on blur

  Tap any name or amount to edit it in place. No edit mode, no form.

[Filing a receipt] ── budget line typeahead
  Type "roof" ─► no match ─► "Create budget line 'Roofing'"  ← inline, stays put
                                                                never leaves the queue

One concept, one word, two places it appears — and in both places it's created inline. Never a page, never a dialog you have to dismiss to get back to what you were doing.

Rabbit hole

Deleting a line with receipts on it. Don't build a re-assignment flow. If a line has receipts, the delete affordance isn't there, and the row explains: "3 receipts use this line." Move them first from the receipts themselves.

No-gos
  • No sub-lines, no nesting, no hierarchy. Flat list.
  • No budget revision history or approval of changes.
  • No standard chart of accounts. Every company names its own lines.

Every day

These three are the product. Everything above exists so these can happen, and everything below exists because these happened.

Snap a receiptLarge batch · 6 weeks

Crew · phone · many times a day · the epicenter
Job story

When a supplier hands me paper and a truck is waiting, I want it into Zimba before I put my phone away, so it can't end up creased in my pocket for three weeks.

What happens today

Musa is at Kitala. 11am, sun overhead, one hand on a delivery note. To record it he must: open the app, tap Projects, find Kitala in a paginated list, tap it, tap New expense, open the supplier dropdown, discover this supplier doesn't exist, leave the page to create one, fill six fields, come back, pick a purchase date, upload the photo, type a description, choose a category, type a quantity, type a unit rate, decide the payment status, and save.

Twenty-plus interactions, two hands, in sunlight, with a truck waiting. He puts the paper in his pocket. It surfaces in three weeks and nobody remembers what it was for.

  • Confirm which project it belongs to
  • Done — and immediately be able to do it again

Rabbit holes

Offline is not optional. Ugandan sites have bad signal and this is where the app lives. The photo writes to local storage immediately, the row appears immediately, and upload retries in the background forever. Musa never sees a spinner, a progress bar, or a retry button. The existing receipt-draft storage already persists File objects, so the primitive exists — but the current fake progress bar stepping 10→45→90→100 and the window.location.reload() after upload both have to go.

Project defaulting. Last used, full stop. No geolocation, no guessing, no "we think you're at Kitala." Cheap, predictable, right nine times out of ten, and obviously wrong the one time it's wrong.

No-gos
  • No OCR. The most tempting feature in this product and the biggest rabbit hole in it. Handwritten Ugandan delivery notes in bad light. Not in v1.
  • No amount field on capture. Not even optional — an optional field is still a decision to make while a truck waits.
  • No editing or deleting a receipt from the capture screen. Wrong photo? Take another.
  • No multi-photo receipts. One photo, one receipt. A two-page invoice is two receipts, filed together at the desk.
  • No notes or voice memo field.
  • No Crew visibility of any money total, anywhere.

When photos have piled up from three sites, I want to turn them into numbers fast and know when I'm finished, so bookkeeping is twenty minutes at the end of the day instead of a weekend in March.

What happens today

There is no queue. /expenses exists but appears in no navigation — not the sidebar, not the mobile bar — so the only ways to reach it are typing the URL or deleting a receipt, which redirects there. Rabbit holes

Inline supplier creation is the whole workflow. If creating a supplier means leaving the queue, the queue never empties. The current ?supplier= round-trip through /suppliers/new and back is exactly the pattern being deleted. A supplier created here has a name and nothing else; phone and email get filled in later, or never.

Multi-line receipts. One amount per receipt covers the large majority. "Split into items" opens the existing line-item editor. It's a rare path, so it's allowed to look plainer than the rest of the app — but it must not shape the default.

Over-budget filing. Never block it. The money was already spent; refusing to record reality is how shadow spreadsheets get born. Warn, record, and let the project page go red.

No-gos
  • No approval step. Filed is filed. Three of the four current status fields exist to serve an approval flow that has no interface.
  • No bulk edit, no multi-select, no "file all as Foundation."
  • No assigning a receipt to a person for follow-up.
  • No tax or VAT fields. When someone needs them we'll ask what they need.
  • No duplicate detection.

Pay a receiptLarge batch · 6 weeks

Manager · desk · weekly
Job story

When Ssemwanga rings asking about his money, I want to see what we owe him and record what I just sent, so the next call starts from the same number we both agree on.

What happens today

Mostly right, actually. Mark fully paid is one click with no confirmation, and Record partial payment opens a dialog. But the payment method is a raw <select> — unstyled, unlike every other dropdown in the app — and the reference field is labelled "Optional" while asking for nothing in particular.

Actions

  • See what's still owed on a receipt
  • Record a payment: amount, date, how it was sent
  • Mark it settled in one action when you've paid it all
  • See every payment already made against it

Interaction

[Receipt]
  Ssemwanga Hardware · Kitala Site · Foundation
  2,400,000                          Part paid
  Paid 1,000,000 · Owed 1,400,000

  "Record a payment"  ──────────────►  [Payment]
  "Mark it paid off"                   ← fills the amount and files it,
                                         one action, no dialog

  Payments
    12 Mar   Mobile money   600,000
    28 Feb   Cash           400,000

[Payment]
  Amount     1,400,000        ← pre-filled with what's owed
  Date       today            ← pre-filled
  Sent by    Cash · Mobile money · Bank · Cheque    ← four buttons, not a dropdown
  Reference  MTN txn or cheque no.                  ← the label says what to type
  "Record payment"  ───────────────►  [Receipt]

Four payment methods as four visible buttons. A dropdown hides options to save space we have, and costs a tap we don't need to spend.

Rabbit hole

Double-submitting a payment. Payments are the one place in Zimba where a duplicate is a real accounting problem. The existing idempotency key on markReceiptFullyPaidAction is the right instinct — extend it to every payment write, keyed on the client.

No-gos
  • No paying several receipts at once.
  • No mobile money or bank integration. You type what you sent.
  • No payment reversal or void. Record a correcting payment, or delete and re-file.
  • No scheduled or promised future payments. No due dates, no ageing report.
  • No attaching proof-of-payment files to a payment.

Find a receipt againSmall batch · 1–2 weeks

Three separate upload implementations. The project-files page hides its input behind a button, shows a fake progress bar stepping 10→45→90→100, then calls window.location.reload(). Real filenames. One upload component. A download button. No page reload.

Rabbit hole

The hardcoded URL rewrite. The files page currently rewrites any URL containing /api/v1/files/ into https://utfs.io/f/{key}. That's a storage provider's domain hardcoded in a React component, and it will break silently. Files serve through one route or the other, decided once, in one place.

No-gos
  • No folders, no renaming, no versioning, no tags.
  • No bulk download or zip.
  • No in-browser PDF preview — download it.
  • No file comments.

  • Delete a receipt

ActionConfirm?

The three bets

Fourteen workflows, three six-week cycles, two-week cool-downs between. Each cycle is a fixed time box with variable scope: if week six arrives and a scope isn't done, it doesn't ship and it doesn't roll over. It gets re-shaped and re-bet, or dropped.

Bet 1 · The Loop 6 weeks

Snap → inbox → filed, end to end, thin. This is the epicenter and it must be built as one vertical slice, because capture without an inbox is a folder of photos and an inbox without capture is empty.

ScopeContains
CaptureW5. Camera, project default, offline queue.
InboxW6 queue. Count, cards, empty state.
File itW6 form. Inline supplier and budget line creation.
Four doorsNav collapse. Inbox becomes home. Crew see two doors.
Two statesFiled / not filed. Delete the other three status fields.

If we run out of time, the first things cut are: split-into-items, keyboard shortcuts, the photo zoom. The last things cut are the camera and the queue. Those two are the bet.

Cool-down · 2 weeks

Delete, don't build. Reports, Analytics, Budget, and Settings routes come out. The project-create draft bug disappears with the wizard. The dead expense filter tabs go. The hardcoded utfs.io rewrite gets fixed. No new features, and nobody is asked to plan the next cycle until the last week.

Budget linesW4. Inline add and edit everywhere.
PaymentsW7. Four method buttons, idempotent writes, payment list.
SuppliersW10. Sorted by what we owe.

ScopeContains

v1 ships at the end of Bet 3. Twenty-two weeks including cool-downs. Not a roadmap — Bet 2 and Bet 3 are sketched here so you can see the shape, but they get properly shaped and re-decided at the betting table before each cycle, because six weeks from now we'll know things we don't know today.

The circuit breaker

If Bet 1 is 70% done at the end of week six, it does not ship and it does not continue into week seven. We re-shape and re-bet it. Extending a cycle once teaches everyone that deadlines are decorative, and after that no estimate in the company means anything.

Master no-go list

Everything Zimba v1 will not do, in one place, so nobody has to go hunting to check. If a decision during a cycle would contradict this list, the answer is no without a meeting.

Won't build

  • OCR or receipt data extraction
  • Approval workflows
  • Multi-currency
  • Purchase orders and quotations
  • Payroll or labour tracking
  • Schedules, dates, Gantt charts
  • Accounting software sync
  • Mobile money or bank integration
  • Push notifications
  • SMS sending
  • Undo, trash, or soft-delete
  • Audit log or edit history
  • Period locking
  • CSV or Excel export
  • PDF generation
  • Per-project permissions
  • Client or contact records
  • Supplier statements
  • Duplicate detection
  • Sample or demo data

Won't have on screen

  • Notification preferences
  • A dashboard of widgets
  • An activity feed
  • Pie, donut, or area charts
  • Decorative sparklines
  • Notification badges, except the inbox count

  • Fake progress bars

Open questions

Four things I can't shape without an answer from you. Each one changes the design, so they're worth answering before Bet 1 starts rather than during it.

1. Why does signup need manual approval?

Every workflow above assumes self-serve signup, and W1 falls apart if that's wrong. If the gate is there for fraud prevention, the answer is different than if it's sales qualification, and different again if it's just leftover scaffolding from an earlier idea. This is the highest-leverage question in the document.

2. Does the foreman actually have a phone that can do this?

The entire capture design assumes an Android phone with a working camera and intermittent data. If the reality is a shared feature phone, or a foreman who sends photos to the accountant on WhatsApp and never opens Zimba himself, then Bet 1 is the wrong bet and we should shape "import from WhatsApp" instead. One phone call to one real foreman settles this.

3. Who files the receipts — one person or several?

The inbox is designed for one person emptying a queue. If three people file concurrently we need to think about two of them opening the same receipt. Not hard, but it's a decision, and it's cheaper to make now than in week four.

4. Is anyone using the four roles?

Collapsing to Crew and Manager is the single biggest simplification here. If a real customer is genuinely relying on accountant being distinct from site_manager, I want to know what they can't do that they'd need to.
